Aeonic Tarot at MakePlayingCards

MakePlayingCards (MPC) prints one tarot deck at a time with no minimum order. To print ours, the deck needs 79 image files, 78 faces and one back, each exactly 897 × 1497 pixels. Our scans need four fixes before they meet MPC's rules, listed under “Rules for our files”.

found means MPC's own pages say it. check means I could not confirm it; look in MPC's design tool. measured means I measured it from our card files.

The short version

  • Print one deck: upload 79 images, choose card stock and a box, and order. One tarot deck is listed at $26.10. Production is about 1–2 weeks, plus shipping. found
  • Order one proof deck first. Screens and card stock never match exactly. Teal and copper are the colors most likely to print darker.
  • Sell it without handling stock: the MPC Marketplace prints, ships and handles customer service. You set a markup on MPC's price. found
  • Our files are not print-ready yet. The 1909 scans have worn round corners, uneven shapes, and frame lines that sit a little too close to the cut. A program in this folder will fix that.

Words used here

Print-on-demand
The printer makes each deck only when someone orders it, so nobody holds stock.
Trim (cut line)
Where the blade cuts. For MPC tarot this is the finished card: 2.75 × 4.75 inches.
Bleed
Extra picture around the outside of the trim. The blade cuts it off. It exists so that a slightly off-center cut shows picture, not a white sliver.
Safe area
The inner zone where text and important lines are sure to survive the cut. Anything between the safe line and the trim might be cut close or unevenly.
DPI
Dots (pixels) per inch. MPC wants at least 300, so a 2.75-inch-wide card needs 825 pixels across.
Proof deck
One copy you order for yourself to check colors, cutting and card stock before anyone else buys.
Markup
What you add on top of MPC's base price when you sell through their marketplace. It is your earnings, minus fees.

Card stock and packaging

  • Smooth: S30 (standard, blue core), S33 (black core, smoother and more opaque).
  • Linen texture: M28 and M29 (blue core), M31 (casino quality, black core). M30 and M32 need 1,000 decks.
  • Other: S20 (thin), A35 (thick), P10 (plastic), E27 (eco).
  • Finish: MPC finish or gloss. Extras include gilt edges and gold, silver or holographic foil.
  • Packaging: shrink-wrap (default), tuck box, rigid box, magnetic book box, tin, or a black or purple velvet bag.

A core is a layer inside the card stock that stops light shining through, so nobody can read a card from its back. A black core blocks more light than a blue one.

A note on uneven borders. The 1909 printing itself has uneven paper borders, which shows in the scans. Every bordered deck from every printer has some cutting drift. The question is only whether our frame lines should sit fully inside MPC's safe line. That choice is listed under decisions below.

Rules for our files

These are what the conversion program must do. Each rule traces back to an MPC rule or to a measurement above.

  1. R1Write each card as a 897 × 1497 pixel image marked 300 dpi, in RGB, as high-quality JPEG or PNG.
  2. R2Find each scan's card edge, remove the scanner background, and fill the worn round corners with the card's own cream paper.
  3. R3Fit every card to the 825 × 1425 cut size without stretching it out of shape. Make up the difference with paper, not distortion.
  4. R4Extend the cream paper 36 pixels past the cut on every side (the bleed), so a drifting blade never shows white.
  5. R5Decide how the frame lines relate to the safe line (see decisions), and apply the same rule to all 78 cards.
  6. R6Make the back from the full-size art. It must look identical after a half turn, so it never reveals a reversed card, and the copyright notice must sit inside the safe area.
  7. R7Name the files in deck order, 00 to 77 and back, so the upload order matches the deck.
  8. R8Produce a check sheet: every card with its cut line and safe line drawn on, to inspect before uploading.

Decisions for Dolores

Frame lines and the safe line

Option A: shrink each picture about 5% inside a wider paper border, so every frame line sits inside the safe line. The cards look slightly more framed than the 1909 originals.

Option B: keep the 1909 proportions. The cards match the originals, and borders may come out a little uneven.

Either can be changed later by re-running the program.

Where the full-size back lives

This repository is public. A full-size back stored here can be downloaded and printed by anyone, copyright notice or not.

Option A: keep the full-size back and the finished print files out of the repository. The program reads the back from a file you supply, as the card-back scripts already do.

Option B: store them in the repository for convenience.

How to sell

MPC Marketplace: MPC prints, ships and handles customers.

QPMN: MPC's sister company. It prints and ships orders from your own Shopify, Etsy, Squarespace or WooCommerce shop.

Bulk order: buy many decks at the lower bulk price and sell them yourself.

Details are below.

Ordering a proof deck

  1. Run the conversion program. It writes 79 files and a check sheet. Look through the check sheet.
  2. Open MPC's tarot cards page and start the design. Choose the card stock, set the number of cards to 78, and set the quantity to 1. check the exact button names.
  3. Upload the 78 faces in order, and use the one back for every card. check whether the tool takes many files at once.
  4. In the preview, check that nothing important crosses the red dotted safe line. If MPC says an image is below print requirements, stop; the file is the wrong size.
  5. Choose the packaging, add the deck to the cart, and check the shipping options and cost at checkout. check import duties for US delivery, which the checkout page should show.
  6. When the proof arrives, check four things: how the colors compare with the screen, whether the borders are even, how the card stock feels while shuffling, and whether the backs give away reversals. Fix anything you find, then reorder.
